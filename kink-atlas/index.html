01

How to bring it up

Do not open with a label. Open with a memory of a feeling.

Bad: “I think I might be into D/s.”

Better: “Remember when you held my wrist in the kitchen? I have been thinking about it for a week.”

Specific beats categorical. Nobody has to have an opinion about a category. Everybody has an opinion about a week ago.

02

Safe words and signals

A safe word is a word that means stop, agreed in advance. It is the thing that lets everything else be loose.

Traffic lights beat a single word, because most scenes are neither fine nor over: green keep going, amber ease off, red stop.

If speech is unavailable, agree a physical signal. Two taps. Or hold an object and drop it.

03

Negotiating, quickly

Three lists, five minutes, both directions: curious about, know you like, hard no. Then agree one thing to try and one to leave alone.

Write them down the first time. Memory is unreliable when you are excited.

04

A first evening that works

Ninety minutes. Ten talking, five setting up, an hour doing one simple thing, fifteen lying around afterwards doing nothing in particular.

Change exactly one variable. One successful evening is one variable.

05

Aftercare, both ways

Aftercare is not a reward for the person who received. Both people come down, often at different speeds, and whoever was in charge frequently crashes harder — they were holding the map, and that is work.

Blanket, water, food, quiet, no decisions for twenty minutes. Then talk about what actually happened, specifically.

06

Drop, and what to expect

A flat, weepy, vaguely hungover feeling arriving anywhere from two hours to two days later. Completely normal, mostly a neurotransmitter hangover.

Worse with hunger, exhaustion, no contact afterwards, and novelty. Plan around all four.

07

Gear, in order of usefulness

One: a scarf. Two: a blindfold that actually blocks light. Three: clothespins. Four: a timer. Five: a soft flogger — the last thing you need and the first thing people buy.

Geometry matters far more than equipment.

08

Worth discarding

That submission is passive. That topping means never being unsure. That a dynamic must be 24/7 or meaningless. That pain and pleasure are opposites. That you need experience before you are allowed to want anything.

Also: that it has to look like anyone else’s version of it.
